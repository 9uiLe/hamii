# Compiler and Index Identity

## Related Decision

[Product Mapping Locator](../../ADR.md): whether a compiler-derived symbol identity can identify an expected-kind Product declaration or resource reproducibly within a pinned Product commit.

## Hypothesis

Swift USRs in a symbol graph can distinguish declarations that share a display name, survive checkout relocation and file moves, and detect renames. Their production use may require Product build settings and an SDK; a USR by itself does not bind a mapping to a Git commit or cover non-Swift resources.

## Questions

- Can the exact pinned source yield a USR without compiling or indexing it, and what toolchain inputs are required?
- Are identities stable across an exact clone and relocation, and what happens after file move, rename, deletion, or commit change?
- Do internal declarations, members, enum cases, overloads, asset catalogs, and expected declaration kinds have usable evidence?
- What are the measured costs and missing guarantees of symbol graphs and IndexStore?

## Prototype Scope

- Read-only pinned, clean Team MINO `dca2202f4be21869190d19bbcf223eb8646a2acd`: `Packages/Domain/Sources/Domain/Entities/Profile.swift` (blob `f15ccde1464aea6db82685fde706b81f47e7f9d5`) and its dependency-free `Domain` Swift package.
- Read-only pinned, clean Apple Food Truck `3954a769e99f3cc53297d94f2b960ceb2665b3d6`: `App/General/FlowLayout.swift` (blob `92c12dc1f8ed077e2384d99792310648b9c8f718`) and `Widgets/Assets.xcassets`.
- Scratch builds, archive relocations, controlled source edits, symlink input, and all compiler outputs stayed under `/tmp/hamii-locator-*`, outside both Product worktrees and hamii. A shared-object Git clone and two `git archive` materializations were made from the exact Team MINO commit. The derived edits are controls, not observations of a Product commit.
- Tooling: macOS 27 arm64; Xcode 27.0 (27A266a), Apple Swift 6.4 `swiftlang-6.4.0.34.1`; separately installed Swiftly Swift 6.4 RELEASE. macOS SDK 27.0. Timings are wall-clock on one machine with changing compiler caches, not Product latency promises.

## Out of Scope

- Full Food Truck Xcode target build, iOS SDK/simulator build, Product resource compilation, parser-based locator comparison, production resolver or Profile schema changes.
- SourceKit `sourcekitd-test` requests: that utility and `indexstore-db` executable were unavailable in the installed Xcode toolchain. SourceKit-LSP build-free USR behavior remains unknown.
- Independent network clone: `git clone --no-local` failed because the local partial/promisor source could not provide an object; a shared-object clone and exact `git archive` relocations succeeded. This does not prove that a self-contained clone can rebuild without obtaining missing objects.

The standalone Food Truck probe is **not** the real Product module. Its `FoodTruckProbe` USRs cannot be claimed as production Product identities. The first Food Truck graph extraction included substantial SwiftUI/SDK loading and cache effects. Do not compare it directly to a warm Domain extraction or infer p95 from these trials.

Primary Swift documentation states, “The Index Store is generated by passing the `-index-store-path` flag” and “it is not efficiently queryable”: [Index Store](https://github.com/swiftlang/indexstore-db/blob/main/Sources/IndexStore/Index%20Store.md). SourceKit-LSP documentation says an index update “essentially type checks the source file”: [Background Indexing](https://github.com/swiftlang/sourcekit-lsp/blob/main/Contributor%20Documentation/Background%20Indexing.md). Local `swiftc -help` exposed `-typecheck` and `-index-store-path`; local `swift-symbolgraph-extract -help` exposed `-module-name`, `-I`, `-sdk`, and `-minimum-access-level`. These sources describe tool behavior; the timings and failures above are local measurements.

## Success Criteria

- At least one exact pinned Team MINO declaration and one internal Food Truck declaration yield a kind-tagged USR; same source at another location yields the same identity.
- Overload and wrong-kind controls distinguish targets; changed source and compiler settings produce explicit evidence rather than a guessed match.
- Required build/index prerequisites and costs are measured, with unavailable paths marked unknown.

## Failure Criteria

- A graph cannot load from pinned source alone, a genuine Product module cannot be identified from the standalone probe, or an untracked/symlinked input can influence compiler output without a pinned-object guard.
- Identity alone treats a new Product commit as the same reviewed source state, or cannot represent resource mapping kinds.

## Result

**Confirmed:** Exact Team MINO source produced `Domain.Profile` USR `s:6Domain7ProfileV`, member `Profile.nickname` `s:6Domain7ProfileV8nicknameSSvp`, and enum case `AvatarColor.red` `s:6Domain11AvatarColorO3redyA2CmF`. A predicate for `Profile.nickname` found one `swift.property` and zero `swift.method` matches, so graph kind metadata can reject that wrong-kind probe. The exact shared-object Git clone and archive relocation yielded the same 566 `(path, kind, precise ID)` triples under the same Xcode/SDK settings. Moving the `Profile.swift` file in a scratch tree preserved `Profile`'s USR but changed its graph `location.uri` absolute path. Thus USR and source location have different relocation behavior.

**Confirmed in controlled edits:** Renaming Food Truck's `FlowLayout` to `FlowLayoutRenamed` changed `s:14FoodTruckProbe10FlowLayoutV` to `s:14FoodTruckProbe17FlowLayoutRenamedV`; deleting that declaration removed it from a new graph. Two added `locatorProbe(_:)` overloads shared a displayed path and `swift.method` kind but had different precise IDs (`…S2iF` and `…S2SF`). Changing only the probe module name changed `FlowLayout`'s USR to `s:20FoodTruckAlternative10FlowLayoutV`. Two scratch Git commits with different OIDs and an added comment still produced the same `Profile` USR. Therefore a USR must be paired with the pinned Product commit and a verified build/module context; it is not itself a commit identity.

**Confirmed safety limit:** Direct `swiftc` compilation through a symlink inside a scratch source root emitted an `EscapeProbe` USR for bytes located outside that root. Compiler output alone does not prove inputs came from tracked, regular blobs in the pinned Git tree. A resolver would need that separate input validation. A source `location.uri` may be absent (Food Truck standalone probe) or an absolute local checkout path (Domain), so it is not a portable source locator by itself.

**Confirmed coverage limit:** The standalone Food Truck graph contained internal `FlowLayout`, its methods and nested `FlowResult.Row`, but zero symbols named `AccentColor`, `AppIcon`, or `WidgetBackground` despite those tracked asset-catalog entries. Compiler Swift USRs cover Swift declarations; this trial provides no identity for asset-catalog resources or arbitrary design token values. A Swift declaration that wraps a token could have a USR, but that does not prove the underlying resource or Native capability semantics.

**Measured:** The exact local samples and trial counts are in the table above and `results.json`. The one-file Food Truck typecheck-only IndexStore took 3.64 s and occupied about 25 MB; three warm Domain no-op builds took 1.299–1.673 s. These are separate operations and cannot be added into a measured end-to-end Product lookup without implementing a reader and pinned-input validation.

**Inferred:** A production compiler-derived locator would need a reviewed Product commit/blob boundary, matching module/build settings, expected declaration kind, and a rule for resources outside Swift declarations. The USR alone cannot supply those guarantees; the controlled same-USR/different-commit and symlink probes show why.

**Unknown:** Whether SourceKit can issue stable USRs from a full unbuilt Product project with pinned build settings; whether a complete Food Truck/iOS graph can be reproduced in supported build environments; USR stability across other Swift/Xcode/SDK versions; complete private/local declaration coverage; cost of building/indexing a whole Product repository; how a production IndexStore reader would bind raw records to the exact Git tree. The Xcode extractor could not read the Swiftly-built module, directly demonstrating a module artifact compatibility constraint, not necessarily a USR-value change.

## Conclusion

Compiler-derived identity is discriminating for the tested Swift declarations, overloads and enum cases, and is independent of checkout location when module/SDK settings are fixed. The tested symbol-graph path required a compiled module. A typecheck-only IndexStore was possible for one isolated source but still required the compiler/SDK and emitted substantial derived data. The evidence does not establish a build-free, environment-independent Product locator or coverage for assets/tokens. Do not decide the ADR from this Spike alone; compare its verified-kind precision and costs against the other locator candidates, and retain commit/blob and tracked-input guards for any compiler-based option.
